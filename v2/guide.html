Calendar user guide

Choose a day to see its Sabbath week and its date in four calendars. The gold cursor marks your selection. On a phone, read the five entries under Selected day.

Try one search

  1. Open the timeline. Under Find by, choose Sabbath number.
  2. Enter 104017 without commas and select Find day →.
  3. Read down the selected day. Sabbath #104,017 falls on Saturday, 3 October 2026 in the Gregorian calendar. The Hebrew row shows 22 Tishrei 5787.

To start with a date you already know, choose its calendar under Find by, fill in the year, month and day, then select Find day →. For a Chinese date, choose regular or leap month. For a Hebrew leap year, choose Adar I or Adar II.

How the week numbers work

Each numbered week runs from Saturday through Friday. Jump to anchor selects Sabbath #1, which the project places on 4 April 33 CE in the Julian calendar (2 April in the Gregorian calendar). Select Previous Sabbath once to reach #0 and again to reach #−1. You can also search for a negative number by typing a minus sign, as in -1.

Move around

  • Today returns to your local civil date. Previous Sabbath and Next Sabbath jump to the Saturday on either side of the selected day.
  • Scale changes how much time you see. Use the arrows beside it to move one day, week, month, year or century at a time. Month, year and century steps follow Gregorian dates.
  • Keyboard: Tab to the timeline. Press ← or → to move one day, or hold Shift with either arrow to move one week.

Understand the date labels

The Hebrew row gives the daytime date. The Chinese row follows the Hong Kong calendar. Both cover Gregorian years 1901–2100. Outside that range, you can still read the Sabbath, Julian and Gregorian rows.

Conversion details explains the calendar conventions and shows the Julian day number (JDN), a single number identifying the selected day across all rows. Use Copy exact JDN if you want to record it.
